Swiss grids and type,
inside Photoshop.

SwissGrid and SwissType are two Photoshop plugins for designing in the Swiss / International Typographic Style: grids in any color, type sizes that lock to the grid, and text that snaps exactly onto the baseline.

SwissGrid

Müller-Brockmann grids in any unit and any color, with type sizes that land on whole lines.

SwissType

Baseline snap, optical edge align, type scales, a line-length checker and text styles.

Install

Both plugins need Photoshop 2023 (version 24.2) or later.

  1. Get the plugin from the Adobe Marketplace: [SwissGrid Marketplace link], [SwissType Marketplace link]. After you buy, the Creative Cloud app installs it for you.
  2. Restart Photoshop if it was open.
  3. Choose Plugins > SwissGrid or Plugins > SwissType. Drag the panel's tab next to your other panels to dock it.

To uninstall: Creative Cloud app > Stock & Marketplace > Plugins > Manage plugins, then Uninstall.

SwissGrid

Build a grid

  1. Open a document. The top line of the panel shows its size and resolution.
  2. Name the grid and pick a color (type a hex value or click a swatch).
  3. Choose how to draw it: Guides + colored layer, Colored layer only or Guides only, and the line thickness.
  4. Choose a unit (mm, px, in, cm, pt or %). You can also type a unit into any field, like 75px or 12pt.
  5. Fill in Columns (left and right margins, number of columns, width, gutter) and Rows (top and bottom margins, rows, height, gutter). Leave Width or Height empty to fit the space. The line under the fields shows the result as you type.
  6. Click Apply grid or press Enter.

Switching the unit converts every field exactly. If you meant to keep the numbers and just reinterpret them, click Keep the numbers instead in the message that appears.

Type sizes for this grid

SwissGrid reads your grid's baseline unit and lists Caption, Body, Subhead, Heading, Title and Display sizes whose leading lands on whole lines. It also tells you how many columns body text should span for a comfortable 45–75 characters per line, following Robert Bringhurst.

Select text layers in Photoshop, then click Apply on a role to set its size and leading. Shift-click Apply to copy the size instead.

Apply matching baseline

After you apply a field grid, click Apply matching baseline. SwissGrid adds the baseline grid that belongs to it: one line per body leading, from the top of the first field to the bottom of the last, in a contrasting color. SwissType snaps text to this grid.

Manage grids

  • On this document lists every grid you've applied. Hide/Show toggles its colored layer and Remove deletes it.
  • Clear all grids removes all guides and SwissGrid layers from the document.
  • Presets: Load fills the form, Apply draws it at once. Save your own with Save preset, and share them with Export… and Import….
  • Every action is a single Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac).
  • Hide the SwissGrid layers before you export your artwork.

SwissType

The top lines of the panel show the document, its guides and the selected text layer's font, size and leading.

Align

Baseline snap

  1. Choose Snap to: Nearest horizontal guide, or Baseline grid.
  2. For a baseline grid, pick it from the Grid menu. Grids applied with SwissGrid are listed first and the baseline grid is picked automatically. Without SwissGrid, choose a preset or type the grid's Top and Unit (line spacing), like 15.24mm and 5.08mm.
  3. Choose a direction: Nearest, Up only or Down only.
  4. Select one or more text layers and click Snap baseline. Each layer's first baseline moves onto a line, and the panel reports how far each one moved. Point and paragraph text both work; other layer types are skipped.

Optical edge align

Click Left, Right, Top or Bottom edge to move the selected text so the letters themselves, not the text box, touch the nearest guide.

Scale

Enter the body size (pt) and the baseline unit (the body leading, in pt or mm). Pick a scale (Fibonacci, traditional, golden ratio, perfect fifth, perfect fourth or major third) and a minimum leading. Every size gets a leading in whole baseline units. Click Apply on a size to set the selected text layers. With Snap baseline after applying on, they also snap to the grid chosen on the Align tab.

Measure

Select a text layer and click Measure selected text. You get characters per line (Bringhurst's alphabet-length method), words per line, the measure in pt and mm, and the measure or type size you'd need for 66 characters. Point text with line breaks also shows a rag chart. Choose Multi-column to judge narrow columns against 40–50 characters.

Styles

Select a styled text layer, name the style and click Save from selected. Font, size, leading, tracking, color, case and alignment are saved. Tick the parts you want, then click Apply on a style to set them on the selected layers. Update replaces a style with the selected layer's settings. Share styles with Export… and Import….

A typical workflow

  1. SwissGrid: apply a field grid, for example 12 columns × 10 rows on A3, then click Apply matching baseline.
  2. SwissGrid: select your headline and click Apply on Display; select body copy and click Apply on Body.
  3. SwissType > Align: with Baseline grid chosen (your SwissGrid baseline is already picked), select the text and click Snap baseline. Then click Left edge so the letters sit exactly on the margin.
  4. SwissType > Measure: check that the body measure lands between 45 and 75 characters.
  5. Hide the SwissGrid layers before exporting.

Questions

Do I need both plugins?

No. Each works on its own. SwissType can snap to your own guides or a baseline grid you type in. Together, SwissType finds SwissGrid's grids automatically.

Where are my grids saved?

Inside the .psd, so they're still listed when you reopen it. Your presets, styles and settings are saved on your computer.

Snap moved my text to the wrong line.

Check the Direction setting and that the right grid is chosen in Align > Grid. Leave text editing mode (press Esc) before snapping.
